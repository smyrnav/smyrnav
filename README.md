## Career Thesis

Modern economies routinely produce abundance and scarcity side by side which means the surplus resources wasted while demand elsewhere goes unmet. The problem that I want to help solve in my career is the inefficient and wasteful business practices that result in environmental damage, cause waste, pollution and loss of biodiversity, enable humanitarian crises and economic suffering. I hope for my contributions to result in healthier business models and more ethical practices that not only prevent the outcomes mentioned above, but actively help mitigate and reverse the damage that already has been done.

In my work, I focus on understanding how raw materials, food, and other physical resources move through economic systems, and on investigating how those systems might be organised differently to make better use of what already exists — reducing waste and unnecessary extraction while finding better ways to connect existing resources with unmet demand. I’m particularly interested in subjects of industrial ecology and symbiosis, circular economy, resource efficiency, and the intersection of logistics, business models, and resource allocation.
